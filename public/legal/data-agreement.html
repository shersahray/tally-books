This agreement is between [Corporation name] inc. (“we”) and the bookkeeping or accounting firm that uses hosted Sumlora to keep its clients’ books (“the firm”). It forms part of the Terms of service. It sets out how we protect the personal information in the firm’s books, as Quebec’s private-sector privacy law (Law 25) and Canada’s PIPEDA require when a business entrusts personal information to a service provider.

1. Who decides, who serves

  • The firm decides. The firm, or its clients, decide what personal information goes into Sumlora and why. The firm remains responsible to its clients for it.
  • We serve. We hold and process that information only to provide Sumlora to the firm, and only as these terms and the firm’s instructions say.

2. What we may and may not do

  • We use the firm’s information only to provide, secure, back up and support Sumlora for the firm.
  • We do not sell it, use it for advertising, or use it to train AI models.
  • We look at the firm’s books only when needed to keep the service running, fix a problem the firm asked us to fix, or meet a legal obligation.
  • Everyone on our side who can reach the information is bound by confidentiality and has access only to what their work needs.
  • We do not keep the information after this agreement ends, except as section 7 says.

4. Service providers we use

We use the service providers listed in our Privacy policy: Microsoft (Azure hosting, in Canada), Anthropic (AI features, in the United States, only when the firm turns them on for a company), Stripe (online payments, only when the firm turns them on) and Plaid (bank feeds, only for banks the firm connects). Each is bound by a contract that protects the information at least as well as this agreement.

We will tell the firm at least 30 days before we add or replace a service provider that receives the firm’s personal information. The firm may object, and may cancel without penalty if we can’t address its objection.

Before information leaves Quebec, we assess whether it will be adequately protected, as Law 25 requires. We share a summary of those assessments with the firm on request.

5. If there is a breach

  • We tell the firm’s main contact without delay, and within 72 hours of learning of a confidentiality incident affecting its information.
  • We tell the firm what happened, what information and which companies are affected, what we have done to limit the harm, and who to contact.
  • We record the incident in our incident register and help the firm notify its clients, the Commission d’accès à l’information du Québec or the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada where required.

6. Requests from people

If someone asks us to see, correct or delete their information in the firm’s books, we pass the request to the firm within 5 business days and don’t answer it ourselves unless the firm asks us to. Sumlora’s tools (editing records, exports, the year-end package) let the firm answer most requests itself. We help with the rest.

7. When the agreement ends

  • The firm has [60] days to download its books and its clients’ books.
  • We then delete them from our servers, and from our backups within a further [30] days.
  • We confirm the deletion in writing on request.
  • We keep only what the law requires us to keep, such as our own billing records, and protect it under this agreement until it is deleted.

8. Checking that we do what we say

Once a year, or after a breach, the firm may ask us for a written summary of our security measures and of any incidents. The firm may ask reasonable questions, and we will answer them within 30 days.
